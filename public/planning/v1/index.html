EVFlow: Planning Presentation

Fair, uncertainty-aware scheduling across every charger on site.

The problem

  • Sites with many EV chargers (campus, office, depot) share a limited power supply.
  • First-come-first-served charging creates queues and unfair access.
  • Uncoordinated charging causes demand peaks and higher electricity cost.
  • Arrival times, departure times and energy needs are uncertain, so static plans fail.

Vision and goals

  • Fairness: every driver receives a guaranteed share of energy before departure.
  • Uncertainty-awareness: plans use ranges and confidence, and adapt as things change.
  • Site-wide control: one schedule across all chargers within the power limit.
  • Transparency: drivers and operators can see why a schedule was chosen.

Stakeholders and users

Drivers

Book a slot, see charging status and expected completion.

Site operator

Monitors load, chargers and fairness reports.

Facility admin

Configures power limits, charger inventory and policies.

Scheduling approach

  • Fairness: max-min fair allocation of power, weighted by remaining energy and deadline.
  • Uncertainty: departure and energy need are modelled as ranges with confidence levels.
  • Robustness: plan against the likely case and keep reserve capacity for surprises.
  • Re-planning: rolling horizon, re-solved at fixed intervals and on every event.
  • Evaluation: compare against first-come-first-served on fairness, wait time and peak load.

Process and tools

  • Agile (Scrum-style) with two-week sprints and a short review at the end of each.
  • GitHub Issues and Projects board for backlog and sprint tracking.
  • Feature branches, pull requests and peer review before merge to main.
  • Automatic deployment to Cloudflare Pages from main; every release is tagged.
  • Testing: unit tests for the engine, API tests and a manual acceptance checklist.
